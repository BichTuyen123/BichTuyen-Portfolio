## Dataset

## Result
The study results found a positive correlation between foreign direct investment (FDI) and exports in Asian countries in general and upper-middle income countries, lower-middle income countries, OECD countries, and emerging countries in particular. Along with improving production capacity, technology, and product quality, FDI also plays a pivotal role in augmenting enterprise competitiveness, opening up many potential opportunities in the export market. However, the study also shows that depending on the socio-economic development context, factors such as economic development policies, investment environment, etc., the impact of FDI on exports may not be uniform across groups of countries. Notably, FDI exerts the most significant impact in upper-middle income countries, whereas its effect is comparatively diminished in emerging economies. In addition to FDI, factors such as economic growth rate, government spending, exchange rate, total population, inflation, trade openness, and FDI also have an impact on exports. Among them, factors such as economic growth, total population, inflation, trade openness, and HDI index all have positive impacts on exports, while exchange rate and government spending have negative impacts on exports in some research samples.

## Insight
First, to promote foreign direct investment (FDI) in the country, it is necessary to build a potential investment environment for enterprises. Each country should implement tax incentives for FDI enterprises, such as reducing corporate income tax in the first years of operation...or exempting or reducing fees related to administrative procedures related to FDI.
In addition to policies to increase foreign direct investment, policies to stimulate GDP growth, improve the Human Development Index (HDI) and promote trade also contribute to improving export performance. To stimulate economic growth, each country should continue to increase investment in developing transport infrastructure systems and investing in, building digital infrastructure and fundamental technologies for transformation to support the export of goods. In addition, with the problem of global energy shortage, investing in sustainable energy sources to ensure the stability of energy supply for manufacturing enterprises also plays an equally important role. Along with socio-economic development policies, each country needs to invest in education, training, and health care programs to improve the HDI index and develop modern technological and technical skills for the workforce. In terms of trade openness, each country needs to actively participate in Free Trade Agreements and new generation Free Trade Agreements to create conditions for domestic enterprises to have the opportunity to participate in potential markets around the world, as well as help consumers access a rich source of quality goods at reasonable prices. In addition, it is necessary to create more trade promotion activities and trade fairs to help enterprises find stable sources of raw materials for production, diversify export markets, and, at the same time, learn, approach, and connect with new markets and partners, take advantage of the benefits of Free Trade Agreements (FTAs), strengthen international market connections, and participate deeply in global value chains.
In addition to export promotion policies, each country needs to have strategies to manage and prevent risks to stabilize the economy. Regarding inflation, it is necessary to improve the effectiveness of coordinating fiscal and monetary policies in managing money supply, interest rates, and balancing the amount of money in and out to maintain price stability. And finally, in parallel with controlling inflation, each country needs to monitor and manage exchange rates to minimize negative impacts on exports, create a flexible foreign exchange trading environment, and help businesses adapt to currency fluctuations.
